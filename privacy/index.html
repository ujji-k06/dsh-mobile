Privacy Policy

Learn how Whaleness uses device permissions, stores local data, and connects to your own gateway.

Effective date: 9 October 2026 · Applies to the Whaleness iOS app (including TestFlight builds) and the Android app

Where your data goes: Whaleness is a mobile client that connects to the DeepSeek Harness Mobile Gateway. The gateway runs on a computer or server that you manage yourself. The conversation content you send in the app first reaches the gateway you have paired with, and that gateway then passes it, according to your configuration, through an API to the relevant large model service provider. The Whaleness developers do not provide a shared chat relay server.

1. Scope and boundaries of responsibility

This policy explains how the Whaleness iOS and Android clients themselves handle data. The gateway you run yourself, and the large model service providers you choose, may have their own rules for data retention, use, and deletion; please review their documentation and privacy policies as well. Whaleness's connection to DeepSeek Harness does not mean that Whaleness represents the relevant service providers.

2. What data we handle, and why we handle it

Pairing and connection information

When you scan a QR code or enter pairing information manually, the app reads the gateway address, pairing code, and similar information in order to establish a connection with the gateway you select. The app also generates a device identifier and sends the necessary authentication information to the gateway. On iOS, the long-term pairing token and device identifier are stored in the Keychain; on Android, this information is encrypted with an Android Keystore key, and the ciphertext is then stored in a non-backed-up directory on the device. Gateway addresses, names, and connection preferences are stored in the local settings of each device.

Conversations, workspaces, and attachments

The messages you send, the images you actively select, and the workspace file, conversation, and task operations you initiate in the app are transmitted over the network to the gateway you have paired with, in order to carry out the functions you request. Depending on your model configuration, the gateway may pass messages, the necessary context, and related attachments through an API to large model service providers. The app also receives and displays the conversation content, task status, and files returned by the gateway.

The app stores some connection settings and conversation summaries locally on the device, and creates caches or temporary files for image attachments and file previews. This data is used to restore the interface and improve the experience; it is not used for advertising targeting.

Camera, photos, and file selection

The camera is used only when you choose to scan a pairing QR code; the scan image is recognized on the device, and the app does not save or upload the camera image. Content you select through the system photo or file picker is used for that operation only when you carry out the corresponding send, preview, or file operation. The pairing information in the QR code is used to connect to the selected gateway.

The Android version uses Google ML Kit to recognize QR codes. Images and recognition results are processed on the device and are not sent by ML Kit to Google; however, ML Kit sends device and app information, installation identifiers, and performance and usage metrics to Google for diagnostics and improvement. For details, see Google ML Kit's privacy information.

The Android version also lets you actively open APK files downloaded from your own workspace. Only when you choose to install such a file does the app take you to system settings so you can decide whether to allow installing packages from this app.

Location, background operation, and notifications

While an agent task is running, the iOS version may request low-accuracy location permission and receive location callbacks in the background, in order to maintain background execution time for the task connection; the app does not save location coordinates, and does not send location coordinates to the gateway or to model providers. In some cases, the app may also use silent audio playback to keep a background task alive; this does not involve microphone recording. If you turn off the relevant permissions, the continued running of iOS background tasks may be limited by the system.

The Android version does not request location or microphone permissions. Only while a user-initiated agent task is executing may it start a foreground connection service and show a persistent notification; the service is stopped when the task ends. Android may also request notification permission, to surface approval requests and task results.

If you allow notifications, task status or conversation titles may appear on device notifications and the lock screen; on iOS, Live Activities may also appear in the Dynamic Island. Please be aware that people nearby may see the information on these device surfaces.

3. Who data reaches

  • The gateway you have paired with: receives connection information, conversation requests, and the content you actively send. The gateway is run and managed by you.
  • The large model service providers you configure: the gateway may send them, through an API, the content needed to fulfill model requests; the specific providers are determined by your gateway configuration.
  • Google ML Kit (Android only): QR code recognition happens on the device; Google receives that SDK's device, app, installation identifier, and performance and usage metrics for diagnostics and improvement, and does not receive the scan image or the recognition result. See the ML Kit data disclosure for details.
  • Apple (iOS TestFlight only): when you install and give feedback through TestFlight, Apple may handle test feedback and diagnostic information under its own service terms.

Currently, neither the iOS nor the Android client integrates any advertising SDK, and neither sells your conversation content for advertising purposes.

4. Retention periods and deletion

On iOS, the pairing token is stored in the Keychain; on Android, the pairing token is encrypted with an Android Keystore key and stored in a local file that is not backed up. When you remove the corresponding gateway profile, the app deletes that gateway's local token; in some authentication failure cases it also deletes the token and requires you to pair again. Connection settings and conversation summaries are stored locally on the device, and ordinary settings may be affected by the operating system's backup mechanisms. On both platforms, the image attachment cache uses a clearable cache directory, and the current version uses a 7-day expiry policy; file previews and downloads may produce temporary files, and the caches may also be cleared earlier by the operating system.

You can remove a gateway profile in the app to delete the corresponding local pairing token; you can also revoke the camera, notification, and iOS location permissions in system settings. To delete conversation or workspace data on the gateway, do so on the gateway or host that you manage yourself. Retention and deletion of API requests by large model service providers is handled under their respective rules; please contact the relevant provider for details. For more information about this client's local data handling, or to request assistance, please contact us at the email address below.

5. Transmission and security

The app supports connecting to the ws:// or wss:// gateway addresses you configure. wss:// provides transport encryption, whereas ws:// does not provide the same level of encrypted protection. If the gateway is reachable from the public internet, prefer a trusted encrypted connection, and protect your gateway and model API credentials carefully. The app stores the long-term pairing token in the iOS Keychain, or encrypts it with the Android Keystore and stores it in a local file, rather than leaving it in plain text in ordinary settings.
